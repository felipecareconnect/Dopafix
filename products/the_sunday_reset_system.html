How to use this system

The Sunday Reset is not about fixing your week. It is about noticing your week, releasing what is done, and preparing for what comes next — with gentleness, not rigidity.

The promise

One hour. One worksheet. No perfection required. If you only complete half the sections, that is still a successful reset.

When to do it

Sunday works for most people, but any day can be your reset day. The key is consistency, not the calendar. Pick a day and protect it like an appointment.

What you need

  • This workbook (printed or digital)
  • A pen or stylus
  • One hour where you won't be interrupted
  • Your calendar or planner from the past week
  • Your to-do list or task app

What you don't need

  • A perfect week to review
  • A detailed plan for the week ahead
  • Motivation or inspiration
  • Guilt about what didn't get done
"The Sunday Reset is not a performance review. It is a compassionate checkpoint. You are gathering data about yourself, not grading yourself."

The Philosophy

Why a weekly reset works better than a daily overhaul.

The philosophy of the reset

Most productivity systems fail because they demand daily perfection. Miss one day and the whole system collapses. The Sunday Reset avoids this by operating on a weekly rhythm — a forgiving loop that gives you seven chances to get back on track.

Why Sunday (or any single day)

Doing a reset every day is exhausting. Doing one once a month is too infrequent to catch patterns. Once a week hits the sweet spot: frequent enough to stay oriented, spaced enough to not feel like another daily chore.

The four movements

01 Review — Look back at the week without judgment.

02 Release — Let go of what is finished, failed, or no longer relevant.

03 Refill — Identify what you need for the week ahead.

04 Preview — Sketch a loose structure for the coming week.

The non-negotiables

  • No guilt. The reset is a mirror, not a judge.
  • No perfection. Half-completed resets are valid resets.
  • No overplanning. The preview is a sketch, not a contract.
  • No comparison. Your reset looks like your life, not someone else's Instagram.
Important: If you are in a depressive episode, recovering from burnout, or dealing with a crisis, skip the reset. Come back when you have capacity. The system will wait for you.

01 — Review

Look back at the week without judgment.

Step 01: Review

The goal of the review is simple: gather data about your week. Not to evaluate. Not to score. Just to notice.

How to review

  1. Look at your calendar, planner, or task list from the past seven days.
  2. For each day, ask: What happened? What felt easy? What felt hard?
  3. Notice patterns, not isolated incidents. One bad day is weather. Three bad days is climate.
  4. Write down what you notice. Even one word per day is enough.
You are not looking for failures. You are looking for friction points — the places where your system rubbed against reality.

Review prompts

  • What was the best moment of this week?
  • When did I feel most in flow?
  • When did I feel most stuck?
  • What did I avoid? Why?
  • What took more energy than it should have?
  • What gave me energy back?

02 — Release

Let go of what is finished, failed, or no longer relevant.

Step 02: Release

The release step is about clearing mental and physical space. Unfinished tasks, abandoned projects, and lingering guilt all take up cognitive real estate. This step gives you permission to let them go.

The three release categories

A
Finished & Forgotten
Tasks you completed but never acknowledged. Write them down. Give yourself credit. Cross them off for real.
B
Failed & Learned
Things that didn't work out. Not failures — data. Write one thing you learned from each. Then let them go.
C
Stale & Irrelevant
Tasks, goals, or commitments that no longer matter. If you have been carrying them for weeks without action, they are probably not real priorities.
"You do not need to finish everything you started. You need to finish the things that still matter. Everything else is clutter."

03 — Refill

Identify what you need for the week ahead.

Step 03: Refill

Before you plan what to do, plan what you need. Energy, supplies, support, boundaries — these are the inputs that make output possible.

The four refill buckets

Physical

Sleep, food, movement, medication, medical appointments. The foundation everything else sits on.

Mental

Information, clarity, focus time, reduced decision load. What do you need to think clearly this week?

Emotional

Connection, solitude, validation, safety. Who do you need around you? Who do you need distance from?

Environmental

Clean space, working tools, stocked supplies, quiet zones. What does your environment need to support you?

"You cannot pour from an empty cup" is a cliché because it is true. The refill step exists because most of us skip it entirely.

04 — Preview

Sketch a loose structure for the coming week.

Step 04: Preview

The preview is not a detailed schedule. It is a sketch — a loose idea of where your energy and attention will go. Think of it as drawing boundaries around your time, not filling every hour.

The preview rules

  • Three priorities maximum. Not ten. Three. Everything else is bonus.
  • Leave white space. Unscheduled time is not wasted time. It is buffer time.
  • Attach tasks to days loosely. "Tuesday or Wednesday" is better than "Tuesday at 2pm."
  • Include one enjoyable thing per day. Even if it is tiny. Even if it is just "good coffee."
The "must / should / could" framework

Must — Has a hard deadline or real consequence if missed. Non-negotiable.

Should — Important but flexible. Can be moved if needed.

Could — Nice to have. No pressure. If it happens, great. If not, also great.

"A good preview gives you direction without trapping you. It is a compass, not a cage."

When You Don't Feel Like Resetting

Protocols for low-energy, high-stress, or "I can't even" weeks.

Low-energy protocols

Some weeks, the full reset feels impossible. That is normal. These scaled-down versions exist for exactly those weeks.

5m
The Micro Reset

For when you have five minutes and zero motivation.

  1. Write down one thing that went okay this week.
  2. Write down one thing you are releasing.
  3. Write down one thing you need next week.
  4. Done.
15m
The Half Reset

For when you have a little energy but not enough for the full process.

  1. Do the Review worksheet. Skip the detailed daily notes. Just one word per day.
  2. Do the Release worksheet. Only category C (stale & irrelevant).
  3. Write your three priorities for next week.
  4. Skip Refill and detailed Preview. Come back to them when you have more capacity.
Skip
The Skip Week

For when you are burned out, grieving, in crisis, or simply done.

  1. Close this workbook.
  2. Do something restorative. Sleep. Eat. Be with people who care about you.
  3. Come back next week. Or the week after. The system will wait.
"Skipping a reset is not a failure. It is a valid response to your current capacity. The reset is a tool, not a tyrant."
21

Reset, don't rebuild

The Sunday Reset is not about fixing your life every seven days. It is about maintaining gentle contact with your own patterns — noticing what works, releasing what doesn't, and heading into the new week with slightly more clarity than you had before.

Some resets will feel transformative. Others will feel like going through the motions. Both are valid. The value is in the repetition, not in any single session.

What to remember

One hour, not one day. The reset is bounded. It does not consume your Sunday.

Gentle, not rigorous. If it feels like a chore, you are doing it wrong. Simplify until it feels supportive.

Data, not judgment. Every week teaches you something about how you work. Collect the data. Adjust the system.

The system waits for you. Miss a week? Two weeks? A month? The reset is still here when you are ready.

"You don't need a perfect week to have a good reset. You just need an honest look at the week you had, and a gentle plan for the week ahead."
